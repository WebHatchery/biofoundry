# Product Brief

## The game

Biofoundry is a colony-management and automation game in which the factory is
alive. The player digs a warren, assigns jobs, places work sites, and balances
food production against a growing population of goblins and specialist
creatures. Workers choose and execute tasks; the player shapes the system
rather than directly commanding units.

The concise promise is:

> Build a living factory beneath the earth, then feed it well enough to wake
> the ancient creature below.

## Player fantasy

The player is the unseen keeper of an unruly underground ecosystem. A good
warren feels less like a row of machines and more like a habitat that has
learned to run itself. The emotional arc moves from precarious improvisation,
through comprehension and control, to the spectacle of awakening the worm.

## Audience

### Primary

Players who enjoy colony simulators, management games, production chains, and
watching autonomous workers solve a plan. They are comfortable reading a few
numbers but should not need genre expertise to survive the opening.

### Secondary

Players drawn by the creature art and compact campaign who would not commit to
an open-ended colony simulator. They need clear goals, forgiving recovery,
and visible cause and effect.

## Design pillars

### Living automation

Every useful action belongs to a creature with a location, appetite, job, and
travel time. Layout and workforce choices matter because nothing important is
abstracted away.

### Food is power

Food production, storage, and consumption form the central readable economy.
A food deficit should resemble a brownout: work slows, priorities become
urgent, and recovery remains possible before workers desert.

### Indirect control with legible consequences

The player assigns, places, queues, and prioritizes. Creatures act. The game
must explain why a chain is stalled and what player lever can change it.

### A compact dramatic arc

The campaign has a beginning, escalation, climax, and finish in one sitting.
Famine teaches recovery, raids test preparation, equipment demonstrates
compounding automation, and the worm is the final economic stress test.

## Phase One product goals

1. Make the first ten minutes understandable without external explanation.
2. Establish one unmistakable critical path from first farm to awakened worm.
3. Ensure failures are forecast, attributable, and recoverable.
4. Make every mandatory action available through visible touch/click targets.
5. Give the campaign a satisfying ending while preserving optional endless
   play.
6. Replace intuition-only tuning with observed playtest evidence.

## Non-goals

Phase One does not add a second biome, procedural campaign map, narrative
dialogue system, multiplayer, modding, achievements, meta-progression,
additional production tiers, or more creature species. Existing late-game
systems may be hidden, deferred, or simplified if they obscure the campaign.

The milestone prioritizes coherence over preserving every prototype feature
in the first session.

## Experience principles

- The player should always be able to answer: “What am I trying to do next?”
- A stalled production node should communicate its missing worker, input,
  output space, route, or unlock.
- The economy should create pressure without requiring a restart to learn its
  basic rules.
- New concepts should appear because the player needs them, not merely because
  the simulation supports them.
- Important state changes deserve both a visible world response and concise UI
  feedback.
- Keyboard shortcuts can accelerate play but never define the only route.

## Success measures

Phase One is successful when observed first-time players meet all of these
targets:

| Measure | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Start a new warren without help | 100% |
| Identify food as the central constraint by minute 5 | at least 4 of 5 |
| Recover from the taught opening food crisis | at least 4 of 5 |
| Explain one stalled production chain correctly | at least 4 of 5 |
| Reach the first secured-warren goal | at least 4 of 5 |
| Complete the campaign in 25–55 minutes | at least 3 of 5 |
| Complete all required actions with pointer/touch only | 100% |
| Encounter a progress-blocking defect | 0 players |

These are milestone gates, not marketing claims. Failed targets produce
backlog work before Phase One can close.
